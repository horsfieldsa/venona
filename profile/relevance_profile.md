# Relevance profile

**This file describes the domain, not a person.** It is read on every run and
injected into the scoring prompt; nothing about any reader is in it, or in the
code. Individual readers tune their own emphasis and follows inside the app,
so this document's job is to define what the domain covers, concretely enough
to score against. Vague profiles produce vague scores.

---

## The domain

Defense technology, read daily from the trade press: what is being built,
bought, tested and fielded across every domain of warfare -- land, air, sea,
undersea, space, cyber and the electromagnetic spectrum. The scoreable signal
is CHANGE: a program moving, money moving, a capability demonstrated, a
vendor winning or losing ground, a requirement or architecture decision that
shifts what gets built next.

## Technology areas, covered evenly

- **Battlefield AI, autonomy and robotics** -- uncrewed systems in every
  domain, autonomy software, human-machine teaming.
- **C2, networks and communications** -- command-and-control modernization,
  tactical networks, data standards and interface mandates.
- **Sensors and ISR** -- radar, EO/IR, RF, acoustic; the processing chain
  that turns feeds into decisions, wherever it runs (on the platform, at the
  edge, or in cloud and enterprise layers -- and moves between those tiers
  are themselves signal).
- **Space** -- proliferated constellations, missile warning and tracking,
  tactical downlink, on-orbit processing.
- **Munitions and fires** -- precision weapons, hypersonics, counter-UAS,
  directed energy, the magazine-depth problem.
- **Cyber and electronic warfare** -- offensive and defensive cyber,
  spectrum operations, jamming and resilience.
- **The industrial base** -- manufacturing capacity, software factories,
  supply chains, and how fast any of it can scale.

## Who buys

- The **military services** -- Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force, Space
  Force -- through their program offices and PEOs; named programs are where
  intent becomes money.
- **Combatant commands** and SOF, whose urgent needs move faster than
  programs of record.
- **Innovation on-ramps** -- DIU, CDAO, service software factories, OTA
  consortia; their solicitations telegraph where the services are going 12-18
  months ahead of a program of record.
- **Allies and partners** -- NATO, AUKUS, FMS cases -- where they drive or
  reveal a US-relevant requirement.

## The vendor landscape

- The **primes** -- Lockheed Martin, RTX, Northrop Grumman, Boeing, General
  Dynamics, L3Harris, Leidos -- and their build-or-buy decisions.
- The **defense-tech entrants** -- Anduril, Palantir, Shield AI, Scale AI,
  Saronic, Skydio and the cohort behind them -- winning slots primes once
  held by default.
- **Commercial technology crossing over** -- space, AI, compute, networking
  firms taking defense work.
- Movement between those camps -- teaming, acquisition, a prime building
  in-house what it used to buy -- reshapes the market faster than any single
  award.

## What to score highly, in rough priority order

1. **Named-program developments** -- an award, protest, milestone, schedule
   change, tier assignment, or architecture and interface decision on a
   program the press tracks.
2. **Money and acquisition movement** -- budget lines, contract vehicles,
   new starts, cuts, reprogrammings; who can now compete that could not
   before.
3. **Capability demonstrated or fielded** -- a test, an exercise result, a
   first fielding, with numbers and named units where the article has them.
4. **Vendor movement** -- winning, losing, teaming, being selected, or
   shipping a capability that overlaps a rival's.
5. **Acquisition mechanics that change speed** -- OTAs, CSOs, middle-tier
   acquisition, the software pathway, ATO reform.
6. **Direction shifts and counter-trends** in any technology area -- edge
   versus cloud, crewed versus uncrewed, exquisite versus attritable mass.
   A broken trend is worth knowing early, and the note must say which way
   the evidence points.

## What the domain does not need

- Pure geopolitics and diplomatic coverage with no program, technology or
  acquisition consequence.
- Personnel, recruiting, benefits, base infrastructure.
- Pure think-tank commentary with no program or contract attached.
- Market-research-report spam ("the global market will reach $X billion").
- Routine earnings coverage of the large primes, unless it names a tracked
  program.

## Scoring guidance

Prefer the item with a number, a name and a date over the item with a theme.
A capability claim with no program, customer, test or dollar figure attached
is marketing, and scores like it. When a story takes a side in a live
technical argument, say so in the note and say which side.
